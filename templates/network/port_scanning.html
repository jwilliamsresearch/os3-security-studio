Port Scanning

What is it? Port scanning is a reconnaissance technique used to discover open ports and services on network hosts.

Scan Types:
  • TCP Connect: Completes full TCP handshake (most reliable)
  • SYN Stealth: Sends SYN packets only (harder to detect)
  • UDP Scan: Tests UDP ports (less reliable but important)
Port States:
  • Open: Service actively accepting connections
  • Closed: Port reachable but no service listening
  • Filtered: Firewall blocking access
Legitimate Uses:
  • Network inventory and asset management
  • Security assessments and penetration testing
  • Troubleshooting connectivity issues
  • Compliance auditing

Scan Information
TCP Connect Scan:
  • Completes full TCP handshake
  • Most reliable results
  • Easily logged and detected
  • Slower than other methods
SYN Stealth Scan:
  • Sends SYN, waits for response
  • Doesn't complete handshake
  • Harder to detect
  • Faster than TCP connect
UDP Scan:
  • Tests UDP services
  • Less reliable responses
  • Slower due to rate limiting
  • Important for DNS, DHCP, etc.
Security Impact
Risk Levels:
  • High - Critical services (RDP, SMB, databases)
  • Medium - Web services, SSH
  • Low - Standard services (HTTPS, DNS)
What Attackers Look For:
  • Unpatched services
  • Default configurations
  • Unnecessary open ports
  • Version information in banners
  • Administrative interfaces
